How to Access Toll-Free Real-Time Voice Bulletins

Anyone in Bangladesh can dial 1090 from any mobile phone (Grameenphone, Banglalink, Robi, Teletalk) 24/7 for automated interactive voice weather warnings, cyclone alerts, and river water level measurements without requiring mobile data or internet access.

National Threat Escalation

The Four Universal Civic Alert Levels

How emergency management commands categorize incoming meteorological and hydrologic threats to mobilize public action.

🟢 Level 1

Advisory / Monitoring

A weather system or low-pressure depression has formed in maritime zones. No immediate hazard to shorelines. Normal vigilance and routine radio checks recommended.

🟡 Level 2

Pre-cautionary Alert

The system is intensifying and moving toward coastal belts. Small marine craft ordered to return to harbor. Community volunteers inspect cyclone shelters and backup rations.

🟠 Level 3

Danger Warning

Severe storm or surging river crest will strike within 12 to 24 hours. Mandatory evacuation begins for pregnant women, children, elderly, and vulnerable embankment dwellings.

🔴 Level 4

Great Danger & Shelter Lockdown

Catastrophic storm surge and super-cyclone wind forces imminent within 6 to 10 hours. All movement outside must cease. All residents must be sealed inside reinforced shelters.

Maritime & Port Directives

Maritime Cyclone Warning Signals Explained (1 to 11)

Bangladesh Meteorological Department standard coastal signaling system used by seaports (Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Mongla, Payra) and coastal districts.

Signal Number Official Name Wind Velocity & Distance Required Civic & Maritime Action
Signal 1 Distant Cautionary Signal Depression formed in distant sea; winds up to 61 km/h. Ships leaving port may encounter stormy weather. Normal civilian precautions.
Signal 2 Distant Warning Signal Deep depression / cyclonic storm formed in deep sea. Ships in deep ocean must navigate with extreme caution; monitor radio bulletins.
Signal 3 Local Cautionary Signal Port threatened by squally weather; winds 51–61 km/h. Small boats must not venture into deep sea and remain close to coast.
Signal 4 Local Warning Signal Port threatened by cyclonic storm; winds 62–88 km/h. All fishing trawlers and vessels must take shelter in port immediately.
Signal 5 Danger Signal 5 Storm moving toward port; will cross coast to the south of the port. Vulnerable coastal populations prepare for evacuation; secure homesteads.
Signal 6 Danger Signal 6 Storm moving toward port; will cross coast to the north of the port. Immediate evacuation of coastal islands and low-lying polders to shelters.
Signal 7 Danger Signal 7 Storm moving directly over or very close to the port. Full evacuation mandatory. High-risk surge levels expected across shoreline.
Signal 8 Great Danger Signal 8 Severe cyclone (winds 89–117+ km/h); crosses coast to south of port. Catastrophic destruction expected. Complete shelter lockdown mandatory.
Signal 9 Great Danger Signal 9 Severe cyclone; crosses coast to north of port. Extensive flooding and roof-destroying gale forces. No civilian outdoor presence.
Signal 10 Great Danger Signal 10 Severe cyclone crosses directly over or near the port with deadly storm surge. Maximum catastrophic devastation. High surge heights exceeding 4 to 7 metres.
Signal 11 Communication Failure Signal Meteorological communications with port broken; severe storm expected. Local administration assumes worst-case landfall scenario independently.
Hydrological Monitoring

River Basin Flood Warning Stages

Defined by the Flood Forecasting and Warning Centre (FFWC) across 109 river monitoring stations on the Jamuna, Padma, Meghna, Surma, and Kushiyara rivers.

📏 Stage A

Warning Level (Satarkota Shonketo)

River water is rising steadily and is within 50 cm of the defined Danger Level gauge marker.

  • Lowland chars and river sandbars begin to experience seasonal water encroachment.
  • Farmers should harvest standing mature paddy and protect grain silos.
⚠️ Stage B

Danger Level (Bipod Shonketo)

River gauge has touched or crossed the historical bankfull flood danger capacity mark.

  • Water overtops riverbanks, flooding village homesteads and village roads.
  • Move livestock to elevated village killas; relocate families to flood shelters.
🚨 Stage C

Severe Flood (Oti Bipod Shonketo)

Water level exceeds 100 cm (1 metre) above the danger level mark.

  • Embankments collapse; major highways and railway lines submerge.
  • Deploy rescue boats for stranded populations; emergency air-drops of drinking water.
Acoustic & Radio Protocols

Civil Defence Siren Patterns & Emergency Radio

When power and mobile cellular networks collapse during a superstorm, acoustic siren horns and battery AM radio broadcasts are the only reliable public notification channels:

  • Continuous 3-Minute Siren: Indicates imminent disaster impact (Signal 7+ or floodwall breach). Immediate movement to shelters required.
  • Intermittent 15-Second Pulsing Sirens: Early precautionary warning (Signal 4–6). Prepare family go-bags and listen to radio broadcasts.
  • Single Long 1-Minute Steady Tone: All-Clear signal indicating the primary danger front has passed and search-and-rescue teams are active.

Verified Emergency Radio Frequencies

Bangladesh Betar National AM: 693 kHz / 819 kHz (operates on high-power emergency generator transmitters).

Bangladesh Betar FM: 106.0 MHz (Dhaka) / 105.4 MHz (Chattogram) / 103.0 MHz (Khulna).

Action Protocol When an Alert Sounds

1
Turn on Battery Radio Immediately

Tune to Bangladesh Betar or regional frequencies for localized evacuation orders.

2
Grab Your 72-Hour Survival Go-Bag

Secure water bottles, dry food, prescription medications, whistle, and waterproof documents.

3
Shut Off Utility Valves

Cut main electrical breaker and turn gas cylinder knobs tightly to prevent fire explosions.

4
Move In Groups to Verified Shelter

Assist seniors, pregnant mothers, and children; never walk through deep water alone.
