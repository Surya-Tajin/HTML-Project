Hazard Category 01

Floods and Flash Floods

From rapid mountain flash floods with zero warning to slow-inundating river basin overflows.

🛡️ Before the Flood

  • Identify elevated community flood shelters and memorize multiple high-ground routes.
  • Elevate valuable electronics, agricultural seeds, and household assets above historic flood marks.
  • Clean municipal storm drains and prepare sandbags around property entrance thresholds.
  • Secure toxic chemicals and fuel cans in elevated waterproof containers to avoid contaminating water.

⚡ During the Flood

  • Cut off main electrical power switches and shut gas cylinders immediately before waters enter.
  • Never walk, wade, or drive through floodwaters—as little as 15 cm of moving water can knock an adult down.
  • Stay out of basements and underground parking structures; they can become death traps in minutes.
  • If trapped inside a building, move to the highest level. Do not enter a sealed attic unless an escape route to the roof exists.

🔄 After the Flood

  • Do not return home until civil authorities officially declare the area dry and structurally safe.
  • Beware of displaced snakes, rodents, and electrical wires hidden under muddy standing water pools.
  • Boil all water vigorously for at least 1 minute or use certified water purification tablets before ingestion.
  • Throw away all food items that made contact with contaminated floodwaters.
Essential Flood Kit Additions

Inflatable life rings, water purification tablets (Halazone), Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS), waterproof pouches for passports/deeds, high-frequency whistle, tall rubber boots, and portable rope line.

Evacuation Directives

When: Evacuate the moment civil alert Danger Level (Bipod Shonketo) is issued, or water rises past boundary markers. How: Move on foot to high-ground shelters along elevated roadways; never attempt crossing swollen culverts in vehicles.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Driving cars or motorbikes across flooded causeways (vehicles float in 30 cm of water).
  • Allowing children to play or swim in flood currents (extreme risk of electrocution and waterborne cholera).
  • Consuming raw tap water or unboiled tube-well water after wellhead submergence.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Carry toddlers in high chest carriers; fit elderly and disabled family members with buoyant safety vests early; unchain all livestock and pets so they are not trapped as waters rise.

Hazard Category 02

Cyclones and Storm Surges

Tropical revolving storms packing destructive winds exceeding 150 km/h and catastrophic tidal surges.

🛡️ Before the Cyclone

  • Trim overgrown tree branches near roofs and power lines to reduce wind shear damage.
  • Board up glass windows or criss-cross them with heavy tape; secure tin roof corrugated sheeting with tie-down wire.
  • Stock non-perishable foods that require no cooking (chira, gur, biscuits) and fresh drinking water.
  • Listen to maritime storm warnings; heed Signal 4 (Local Warning) and Signal 7–10 (Great Danger).

⚡ During the Cyclone

  • Remain strictly inside designated reinforced concrete cyclone shelters or an interior windowless room.
  • Do not leave shelter when the eye of the storm passes (the deceptive calm); extreme reverse gales follow immediately.
  • Keep battery radio tuned to emergency broadcast stations for official landfall updates.
  • Protect your head with mattresses or heavy blankets if structural roof failure begins.

🔄 After the Cyclone

  • Watch for severed high-voltage power lines dangling in puddles or wrapped around debris.
  • Do not enter coastal areas affected by storm surge until flood levels recede completely.
  • Assist emergency responders in locating trapped neighbours using acoustic whistle signalling.
  • Report severed gas lines and structural damage to local disaster management teams immediately.
Cyclone Specific Emergency Kit

Heavy-duty tarpaulins, nylon utility ropes, thick protective gloves, waterproof flashlights with spare bulbs, dry matches in wax-sealed jars, battery AM radio, and essential family medical doses.

Evacuation Directives

When: The moment Signal 7 or higher is hoisted, immediate mandatory evacuation to fortified cyclone shelters is required. How: Walk in bonded family groups using ropes along predetermined embankment ridges before surge heights rise.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Delaying evacuation to guard household possessions or cattle at coastal embankments.
  • Venturing outside during the cyclone eye calm thinking the storm has passed.
  • Touching metallic fences or corrugated tin sheds during or after the storm.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Escort pregnant mothers, infants, and seniors to the nearest cyclone shelter at Signal 4. Ensure designated pet carriers or release livestock to high-embankment killas (raised earth mounds) with fodder.

Hazard Category 03

Earthquakes

Sudden, violent crustal tremors that occur without warning, triggering structural fractures and falling debris.

🛡️ Before the Earthquake

  • Anchor tall bookshelves, heavy mirrors, gas cylinders, and water heaters firmly to structural wall studs.
  • Identify safe spots in every room: under sturdy wooden tables or desks, against interior load-bearing walls.
  • Keep heavy objects on lower shelves to prevent them from becoming falling projectiles.
  • Conduct regular "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" household and classroom drills.

⚡ During the Earthquake

  • DROP to your hands and knees immediately to prevent being knocked down.
  • COVER your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk. If no shelter is nearby, cover against an interior wall.
  • HOLD ON to your shelter until shaking stops completely; be prepared for it to shift.
  • If outdoors: move to a clear open area away from buildings, glass windows, streetlights, and power cables.

🔄 After the Earthquake

  • Expect dangerous aftershocks. Each aftershock can bring down weakened structures.
  • Evacuate the building via stairs only. NEVER USE ELEVATORS under any circumstances.
  • Check for gas leaks by smell. If gas is suspected, shut off the valve and do not operate electrical switches or open flames.
  • Check yourself and neighbours for injuries; apply direct pressure to any bleeding wounds.
Earthquake Kit Essentials

Sturdy leather work gloves, dust masks (N95 or particulate), safety goggles, loud metal whistle, crescent wrench (for gas/water shut-off), and hard-soled boots kept right beside your bed.

Evacuation Directives

When: Evacuate only AFTER ground tremors stop. How: Move calmly down emergency stairwells. Do not rush panic exits. Assemble in designated open parks or sports fields away from glass facades.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Running outside while the building is actively shaking (falling glass and parapets are the #1 cause of fatalities).
  • Standing inside doorways (modern doorways are not stronger than the rest of the house and do not protect from flying debris).
  • Igniting matches or lighters after the tremor before verifying gas line integrity.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Shield children and wheelchair users under desks; lock wheelchair wheels and cover heads with cushions. Keep leashes near exits for pets who may run away in panic.

Hazard Category 04

Landslides and Hill Collapses

Catastrophic downward movements of soil, rock, and saturated mud on steep hilly terrains during intense monsoons.

🛡️ Before the Landslide

  • Learn your hillside risk history: slopes stripped of vegetation or cut for informal housing are extremely dangerous.
  • Watch for warning signs: doors or windows sticking, new cracks in foundations, leaning trees, or utility poles.
  • Notice sudden changes in water flow: streams turning muddy, or sudden loss of water flow that indicates an upstream debris blockage.
  • Evacuate immediately when monsoon downpours exceed 100 mm in a 24-hour cycle.

⚡ During the Landslide

  • Listen for unusual rumblings, cracking trees, or boulder impacts. Debris flows can move at 50 km/h.
  • If sudden movement occurs near your dwelling, move quickly out of the path to stable higher ground.
  • If escape is impossible, curl into a tight ball and protect your head with your arms and thick bedding.
  • Never cross a river valley or ravine if you suspect a debris flow upstream.

🔄 After the Landslide

  • Stay away from the slide area. Additional slope failures frequently follow the initial collapse.
  • Check for trapped and injured persons near the slide perimeter without entering the unstable mud zone.
  • Report broken utility lines and cracked roadways to municipal emergency authorities immediately.
  • Re-plant damaged slopes with deep-root native vetiver grass and trees to stabilize surrounding soil.
Landslide Emergency Kit

High-visibility emergency vest, heavy-duty crowbar, shovel, battery headlamp, thermal emergency blanket, waterproof footwear, and personal emergency cash.

Evacuation Directives

When: Evacuate hill slopes the moment heavy rain alerts are sounded by authorities. How: Move perpendicular to the slope fall line, toward certified low-risk civic shelters.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Building homes directly at the toe or crest of un-retained steep soil slopes.
  • Sleeping on lower floors of hillside homes during torrential continuous nighttime rains.
  • Returning to a slide site to retrieve belongings while soil remains waterlogged.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Proactively relocate seniors and persons with mobility impairments to flatland relative residences prior to peak monsoon season.

Hazard Category 05

Lightning and Severe Thunderstorms

Intense electrical discharges capable of exceeding 300 million volts, responsible for hundreds of rural and open-field fatalities annually.

🛡️ Before the Storm

  • Follow the 30/30 Rule: If time between seeing lightning and hearing thunder is under 30 seconds, seek substantial shelter immediately.
  • Check weather bulletins before heading into open agricultural fields, river journeys, or sports grounds.
  • Install copper lightning arresters with dedicated earth ground rods on roof parapets.
  • Unplug sensitive home electronics and television antennas before storm cells arrive.

⚡ During the Storm

  • Seek shelter inside a fully enclosed substantial building or a metal-topped hardtop vehicle.
  • NO OPEN SHELTERS: Corrugated tin sheds, bus stops, open pavilions, and thatch huts offer ZERO protection.
  • If caught in an open field with no shelter: crouch down on the balls of your feet, tuck head in, hands over ears. Never lie flat!
  • Stay completely away from isolated tall trees, metal fences, cell towers, and bodies of water.

🔄 After the Storm

  • Remain inside safe shelter for at least 30 minutes after hearing the last sound of thunder.
  • If someone is struck by lightning: IT IS SAFE TO TOUCH THEM IMMEDIATELY. Victims carry no residual electrical charge!
  • Check breathing and pulse immediately; begin CPR without delay if victim is unresponsive.
  • Call 999 for urgent paramedic transport; treat shock with thermal blankets.
Lightning Safety Essentials

Rubber soled footwear, surge protection power strips for homes, portable weather radio, and printed CPR resuscitation reference card.

Evacuation Directives

When: The moment thunder is heard in the distance. How: Exit open agricultural fields and bodies of water immediately; move into brick or concrete buildings.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Taking refuge under isolated tall trees or solitary palm trees (classic lightning target).
  • Holding metal umbrellas, farming implements, or fishing rods during lightning activity.
  • Believing that lightning victims carry an electrical charge and hesitating to perform CPR.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Bring working children and farming youth indoors at the first rumble of thunder; keep frightened domestic pets inside quiet rooms.

Hazard Category 06

Severe Heatwaves

Prolonged spells of abnormally high ambient temperatures above 38°C–42°C with high humidity, creating life-threatening heatstroke risks.

🛡️ Before the Heatwave

  • Insulate home ceilings and hang thick light-coloured curtains or bamboo blinds over sun-facing windows.
  • Prepare multiple water cooling containers, Oral Rehydration Salt (ORS) packets, and lemon electrolytes.
  • Identify air-conditioned or well-shaded community public cooling centres in your district.
  • Reschedule strenuous outdoor labor, sports, and construction tasks to early morning or late evening.

⚡ During the Heatwave

  • Drink plenty of clean water and electrolyte fluids continuously, even if you do not feel thirsty.
  • Avoid sugary sodas, strong tea, coffee, and alcohol, which exacerbate bodily dehydration.
  • Wear loose, lightweight, light-coloured cotton clothing and wide-brim hats or umbrellas when stepping outside.
  • Watch for heatstroke signs: confusion, hot red dry skin, lack of sweating, rapid pulse, and fainting. Treat as medical emergency!

🔄 After the Heatwave

  • Continue gradual hydration to restore internal mineral and electrolyte balance safely.
  • Check on isolated elderly neighbours, infants, and chronic kidney or cardiovascular patients.
  • Check food supplies: hot weather accelerates perishable food spoilage and bacterial food poisoning.
  • Maintain tree canopies around your dwelling to create natural microclimate cooling for future seasons.
Heatwave Support Kit

Reusable insulated water bottles, multiple boxes of WHO-standard ORS, handheld battery fans, clinical thermometer, wide-brim sun hats, and wet cooling towels.

Evacuation / Cooling Transfer

When: Body core temperature exceeds 39°C or heat exhaustion symptoms (dizziness, nausea, clamminess) develop. How: Move immediately to air-conditioned medical clinics or shaded cool rooms.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Leaving children or pets unattended inside locked vehicles for even 2 minutes (temperatures reach 60°C).
  • Consuming unsafe street drinks, contaminated crushed ice, or unpasteurized syrups.
  • Giving extremely cold ice water to a heatstroke patient (can trigger fatal peripheral vasoconstriction and shivering).
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Keep infants out of direct sunlight; hydrate nursing mothers frequently; provide continuous fresh drinking bowls and shaded cool flooring for pets and livestock.

Hazard Category 07

Cold Waves and Dense Fog

Abrupt Arctic or Himalayan cold air incursions dropping temperatures below 6°C–10°C, triggering respiratory illnesses and hypothermia.

🛡️ Before the Cold Wave

  • Insulate draughty doors and window frames with old blankets or plastic sealing film.
  • Gather layered woolen garments, thermal innerwear, socks, woolen caps, and thick blankets for family members.
  • Stock adequate supplies of warm nutritious meals, lentils, and hot herbal teas.
  • Check condition of vehicle fog lights and emergency heaters before travelling on highways.

⚡ During the Cold Wave

  • Dress in multiple loose, lightweight layers—trapped air between clothing layers insulates far better than a single heavy coat.
  • Always wear a warm cap and scarf: up to 30% of bodily heat loss occurs through an uncovered head and neck.
  • Keep elderly family members and newborns in warm interior rooms; monitor for signs of hypothermia (slurred speech, shivering, drowsiness).
  • Exercise extreme caution when driving through dense morning fog: keep low-beam headlights on and reduce speed by half.

🔄 After the Cold Wave

  • Air out rooms and bedding in direct sunlight as soon as fog clears to eliminate dampness and mould.
  • Monitor children for pneumonia and asthmatic bronchial infections; seek clinic attention early.
  • Inspect pipes and water lines for blockages or burst sections in cold-exposed outdoor setups.
  • Donate clean warm winter garments to local community shelter drives for disadvantaged labourers.
Cold Weather Emergency Kit

Mylar thermal space blankets, woolen caps, thick wool socks, petroleum jelly/moisturizers for chapped skin, thermal flasks for hot liquids, and cough/fever medications.

Evacuation Directives

When: When indoor temperatures drop dangerously low and housing lacks windbreaks. How: Move vulnerable seniors to heated municipal community welfare halls.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Burning charcoal, wood coals, or gas stoves inside sealed, unventilated bedrooms (fatal Carbon Monoxide poisoning).
  • Rubbing frostbitten or numb extremities vigorously (causes severe tissue damage).
  • Driving with high-beam headlights on in dense fog (blinds oncoming traffic with backscatter glare).
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Ensure infants sleep in warm sleep-sacks rather than heavy suffocating loose quilts; provide warm straw bedding for outdoor cattle and pets.

Hazard Category 08

Building Fires and Smoke Inhalation

Rapidly spreading structural fires fuelled by electrical short circuits, gas leaks, and flammable materials, producing lethal toxic smoke.

🛡️ Before the Fire

  • Install working smoke alarms on every floor and test their batteries every six months.
  • Keep multi-purpose ABC dry chemical fire extinguishers on each floor and master the PASS method (Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep).
  • Map out at least two independent escape routes from every room and ensure window security grilles have an operable emergency release latch.
  • Never overload electrical wall multi-plugs; replace frayed appliance cords immediately.

⚡ During the Fire

  • GET OUT AND STAY OUT: Evacuate immediately! Never waste time collecting laptops, jewellery, or passports.
  • CRAWL LOW UNDER SMOKE: Toxic gases and superheated smoke rise to the ceiling; clean breathable air is 30–60 cm above the floor.
  • Feel closed doors with the back of your hand before opening: if the door or doorknob is hot, do not open it—use your alternate escape route.
  • If your clothes catch fire: STOP, DROP, AND ROLL immediately to smother the flames.

🔄 After the Fire

  • Assemble at your predetermined exterior meeting point and take a full family headcount.
  • Dial 999 or local Fire Rescue (16163) immediately from outside the structure.
  • Never re-enter a burning or extinguished building until the Fire Marshall certifies it structurally sound.
  • Seek immediate medical evaluation for smoke inhalation, coughing, or soot around nostrils even if you feel fine initially.
Fire Safety Kit

ABC Dry Chemical fire extinguisher, emergency escape smoke hood / particulate respirator, bright LED flashlight, flame-resistant emergency ladder for upper floors, and loud battery smoke alarm.

Evacuation Directives

When: The instant smoke or flames are detected. How: Exit via stairwells; close doors behind you to slow flame spread. Never use elevators. If trapped, seal door gaps with wet towels and signal at windows.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Using elevators during a fire evacuation (power failure will trap occupants in smoke-filled shafts).
  • Pouring water on kitchen grease or oil frying fires (triggers massive fireball explosion; use a metal lid or fire blanket).
  • Re-entering a burning home to rescue pets or heirlooms.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Assign designated adults to assist elderly or disabled individuals down stairs; teach children never to hide under beds or in closets from firefighters.

Hazard Category 09

Severe Droughts and Water Scarcity

Prolonged absence of precipitation resulting in depleted groundwater aquifers, crop failures, and severe water shortages.

🛡️ Before the Drought

  • Install household rainwater harvesting systems with multi-stage sand-and-charcoal filtration tanks.
  • Fix all leaking plumbing faucets, toilets, and pipe joints; a dripping tap wastes up to 30 litres daily.
  • Adopt drip irrigation techniques and mulch soil beds to retain valuable moisture for home vegetable patches.
  • Choose drought-resistant native crop varieties and indigenous fruit-bearing trees.

⚡ During the Drought

  • Prioritize water use strictly for drinking, food preparation, and personal medical hygiene.
  • Reuse greywater from laundry and rice rinsing to flush latrines and hydrate non-edible plants.
  • Never drink untreated agricultural run-off or stagnant pond water without thorough boiling or chemical disinfection.
  • Report illegal municipal water theft or broken distribution mains to local water authorities.

🔄 After the Drought

  • Gradually restore community groundwater reserves by expanding community recharge ponds.
  • Test tube-well water for elevated arsenic or mineral concentration that often spikes during prolonged dry periods.
  • Support community watershed protection zones and community tree-planting barriers.
  • Review household water usage metrics and retain water-saving habits long-term.
Drought Kit & Water Supplies

Food-grade water storage drums with sealed lids, ceramic water filter candles, water purification drops, moisture retention mulch, and manual hand-crank tube-well spares.

Community Water Protocol

When: Groundwater levels drop below pump intake depths. How: Coordinate with municipal emergency water bowsers and share communal deep tube-well rosters equitably.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Using hosepipes to wash cars, pathways, or courtyards during critical municipal water shortages.
  • Drinking unpurified ditch or shallow canal water due to desperation (causes cholera and amoebic dysentery outbreaks).
  • Hoarding community water supplies and inflating prices during crises.
Vulnerable Populations & Pets

Ensure pregnant women and small children receive sterilized clean drinking water first; place shaded shallow water troughs for village cattle and community birds.
