About SafeHorizon

SafeHorizon is an independent civic safety initiative dedicated to disaster survival education, rapid medical emergency triage, climate adaptation literacy, and vulnerable population protection.

Public Protection Mandate

Why SafeHorizon Was Built

When catastrophic disasters strike, civil communication networks fail and municipal rescue teams can take hours or days to reach remote floodwaters or collapsed structures.

In those critical hours, the immediate survival of an injured family member or stranded community depends entirely on what everyday citizens know and do before professional first responders arrive.

SafeHorizon transforms complex clinical directives and meteorological bulletins into clear, accessible, actionable guidelines that any parent, teacher, student, or community volunteer can understand and execute calmly under intense stress.

Guiding Safety Philosophy

Our Four Ethical Pillars

Every sentence and guideline on SafeHorizon is governed by rigorous clinical ethics and humanitarian principles.

🩺 Pillar 1

Clinical Safety & Humility

We strictly discourage untrained individuals from attempting dangerous heroics or uncertified surgical procedures. We teach basic life-support and wound stabilization only, always prioritizing urgent referral to certified hospital trauma care.

📡 Pillar 2

Zero Speculative Forecasting

SafeHorizon does not publish synthetic predictions or unverified computer weather models. We honor official meteorological and hydrological observation bodies, directing the public to verified government disaster signals.

⚡ Pillar 3

Universal Zero-Script Resilience

Built entirely in lean HTML5 and CSS3 without heavy JavaScript dependencies, our platform loads instantly on 2G mobile networks, low-battery devices, and damaged cellular infrastructure during actual disaster events.

🤝 Pillar 4

Universal Open Access

Life-saving knowledge belongs to every human being. SafeHorizon is 100% free, devoid of commercial tracking, pop-up advertisements, and corporate sponsorships.

Essential Answers

Emergency Preparedness & First Aid FAQs

Direct clinical and operational answers to common misconceptions during crises.

Q1: Why should I NEVER apply toothpaste, butter, or ice to a burn?

Applying toothpaste, oil, butter, or raw eggs traps heat within the dermal tissues, allowing the thermal burn to cook deeper flesh layers. Domestic substances introduce dangerous bacteria directly into non-intact skin. Ice, on the other hand, causes severe vasoconstriction and frostbite necrosis. The ONLY clinically proven first response is gentle, cool running tap water for a full 20 minutes continuously.

Q2: Is there really no 24-hour waiting period to report a missing child?

Yes, absolutely! The concept of waiting 24 or 48 hours is a dangerous urban myth. Under national civil law and international child protection protocols, police must immediately accept a missing person report (General Diary) for any child, disoriented elderly individual with dementia, or vulnerable person. Dial 999 or 1098 within the first 60 minutes.

Q3: Can I touch someone who has been struck by lightning?

YES, IMMEDIATELY! A human body does NOT retain or store electrical charge after a lightning strike. It is completely safe to touch the victim instantly to check breathing and deliver life-saving CPR. Hesitation caused by this myth has led to hundreds of preventable fatalities.

Q4: Why does SafeHorizon avoid using JavaScript?

During hurricanes, superstorms, and grid collapses, mobile devices operate in extreme low-power modes with degraded 2G connectivity. Websites reliant on heavy JavaScript frameworks frequently crash or fail to render offline caches. Pure HTML5 and CSS3 guarantee maximum compatibility across every browser, screen reader, and low-end mobile phone.

Q5: Why should snakebites never be cut or sucked with the mouth?

Cutting a bite wound with razor blades severs major blood vessels, causing catastrophic hemorrhage and deep infection, while venom has already entered systemic lymphatic circulation within seconds. Sucking venom with the mouth is ineffective and poisons the rescuer through microscopic mouth sores. The correct procedure is to immobilize the limb with a splint and transport immediately to a hospital with Anti-Venom Serum.

Q6: What should I do if trapped in a burning high-rise?

Never use elevators. If stairwells are blocked by thick smoke, return to your apartment, close all doors, and seal cracks around doors with wet bedsheets and towels. Dial 999 or 16163 to report your exact floor and apartment number. Open a window slightly for fresh air and signal rescuers using a bright flashlight or brightly colored cloth.
