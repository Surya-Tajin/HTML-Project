Missing Persons Safety Network

When a child, an elderly relative with dementia, or a family member goes missing during everyday life or natural disasters, every second matters. Follow the Golden Hour search protocol and submit verification details.

File Emergency Report Form First 60-Minute Checklist

Legal Fact: The "24-Hour Wait Rule" Is a Dangerous Myth

There is no mandatory waiting period of 24 or 48 hours before law enforcement can accept a missing person report in cases involving children, teenagers, elderly persons with cognitive decline, or individuals who disappear in suspicious circumstances. Demand an immediate General Diary (GD) and local police station broadcast within the first hour!

Immediate Rapid Triage

The "Golden Hour" Action Checklist

What family members and caretakers must execute methodically in the first 60 minutes after realizing a loved one is missing.

🔍 Step 01

Immediate Radius & Hazard Sweep

Search the home and immediate surroundings thoroughly. Check closets, under beds, inside vehicles, washing machines, and storage chests.

Critical: Check high-risk water hazards first—open garden ponds, uncovered drains, construction water pits, rivers, and nearby wells.

📞 Step 02

Call 999 & Child Helpline 1098

Contact the nearest Thana (Police Station) and dial 999. Inform the operator of the person's age, exact last-seen coordinate, and specific health vulnerabilities.

Request an immediate transmission over police wireless networks to all patrol vehicles in the sector and transit gateways.

📸 Step 03

Assemble Digital Identity Dossier

Locate the most recent clear, high-resolution color photograph (taken within the last 6 months).

Record the exact clothing worn: color of shirt, pants, footwear, and any distinctive jewelry, glasses, birthmarks, surgical scars, or speech patterns.

Special Protection Protocols

Targeted Search Frameworks

Tailored recovery workflows based on the age, cognitive profile, and environmental circumstances of the missing individual.

👶 Missing Children Protocol

If a child goes missing in a public venue (train terminal, shopping market, religious gathering):

  • Alert facility security to lock down all exit doors immediately and announce a Code Adam description over the PA system.
  • Post trusted relatives at every exit gate to examine everyone leaving with a small child.
  • Ask other children who were playing with them what they last saw without leading their answers.
  • Preserve the child's unwashed clothing, pillowcase, or hairbrush in a clean plastic bag for search-and-rescue canine scent tracking.

🧓 Elderly with Dementia / Alzheimer's

Dementia-related wandering poses severe risks of dehydration, hypothermia, and traffic injury:

  • Understand that wanderers usually travel in the direction of their dominant hand, rarely turning around.
  • Search locations of past emotional significance: former childhood residences, old workplaces, or favourite temples/mosques.
  • Check local hospitals and clinics for unidentified elderly admissions.
  • Equip vulnerable seniors with wearable stainless-steel ID wristbands inscribed with emergency family phone numbers.

🌪️ Disaster & Cyclone Separation

During mass evacuations to cyclone shelters or flood rescue boat loading:

  • Register all family members with the camp camp-commandant or Red Crescent volunteer desk upon entry.
  • Pin waterproof ID tags containing full name, village, and relative phone numbers into children's clothing linings before storms hit.
  • Establish a secondary family meeting landmark (e.g., district high school) in case the primary homestead is destroyed.
  • Check the regional Red Crescent Family Links (RFL) missing bulletin board daily.

Public Protection Advisory

Safeguarding Against Exploitation & Extortion Scams

Families of missing individuals are frequently targeted by malicious extortionists claiming to hold their loved one. Protect your family with these strict security practices:

  • Never Transfer Money: Never send bKash, Nagad, or bank deposits to anonymous callers claiming to have found your relative. Require verified police confirmation at a local police station.
  • Do Not Post Private Phone Numbers on Public Flyers: Route public tip flyers through the official Police Thana number or verified NGO hotlines.
  • Keep Distinguishing Details Secret: Keep one non-public physical detail (such as a specific birthmark or dental shape) undisclosed to test caller veracity.
  • Transit Hub Surveillance: Monitor bus terminals, launch ghats, and border checkpoints, which are common transit corridors for human trafficking.
