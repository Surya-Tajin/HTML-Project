Critical Emergency Directives & First Care

Emergency Help Centre

Standardized, evidence-based immediate action protocols for 14 life-critical emergency situations. Prioritize professional emergency dispatch, prevent secondary trauma, and deliver certified basic stabilization safely.

Call Emergency Services (999) Select Emergency Situation

Important Clinical Safety Notice

Untrained bystanders must NEVER attempt complex invasive medical or dangerous rescue operations. SafeHorizon provides basic stabilization guidance only. In situations involving severe arterial bleeding, third-degree burns, electric shock, chemical poisoning, suspected heart attack, stroke, or unconsciousness, always prioritize calling emergency medical services (999) immediately before any other action.

Standardized Emergency Triage Protocols

Follow these structured protocols strictly. Maintain safety for yourself first before approaching any casualty.

1

Road Accidents and Traffic Injuries

High-energy impact trauma, vehicular entrapment, and highway collisions.

Trauma Alert
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Secure the Perimeter: Park hazards, place reflective triangles 50 metres back, and turn off crashed vehicle ignitions to prevent fire.
  • Call 999 Immediately: Provide the exact highway mile marker, number of vehicles, and approximate casualty count.
  • Keep Casualties Immobile: Keep the victim's head and neck completely still in the neutral position found.
  • Control life-threatening external bleeding by pressing sterile cloth directly over wounds.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER pull or drag an injured victim from a vehicle unless there is an immediate fire or sinking hazard (prevents irreversible spinal paralysis).
  • NEVER remove a motorcyclist's crash helmet unless they have stopped breathing and airway access is impossible.
  • NEVER offer food, water, or cigarettes to injured victims (interferes with emergency surgery anesthesia).
When to Contact 999: Immediately upon witnessing the collision.
Professional Care Threshold: All high-speed collisions, vehicle rollovers, pedestrians struck by vehicles, or any loss of consciousness.
2

Heart Attack and Suspected Stroke

Cardiovascular arterial blockage and cerebrovascular infarction.

Critical Time Window
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Heart Attack: Seat the person comfortably on the floor in the "W-position" (knees bent, back supported) to reduce cardiac workload. Loosen restrictive collar and waist garments.
  • If the victim is not allergic, recommend chewing one standard non-coated adult Aspirin (300 mg) while awaiting ambulance.
  • Stroke — Test FAST:
    • F (Face): Ask them to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
    • A (Arms): Ask them to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward?
    • S (Speech): Ask them to repeat a simple phrase. Is speech slurred or strange?
    • T (Time): If ANY sign is present, dial 999 immediately. Note the exact time symptoms started.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER allow a person having chest tightness to walk around or drive themselves to the hospital.
  • NEVER administer Aspirin to a suspected stroke patient (if the stroke is hemorrhagic, Aspirin will worsen brain bleeding).
  • NEVER wait more than 5 minutes hoping symptoms "will go away."
When to Contact 999: Within the first 60 seconds of chest crushing pain or failed FAST stroke test.
Professional Care Threshold: Every suspected cardiovascular event is a mandatory intensive hospital admission.
3

Severe Bleeding and Minor Cuts

Arterial spurting, deep lacerations, and capillary trauma.

Immediate Pressure Required
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Severe Bleeding: Apply firm, continuous direct pressure over the bleeding wound using a sterile pad or clean cloth for at least 10 minutes without lifting to check.
  • Elevate the injured limb above heart level if no bone fracture is suspected.
  • If blood soaks through, add another pad directly on top. Do not remove the initial pad as it disrupts early clot formation.
  • Minor Cuts: Clean under clean running water, pat dry with sterile gauze, apply antiseptic, and cover with a sterile adhesive dressing.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER remove large embedded objects (knives, glass shards, metal nails). Stabilize the object with bulky bandages around it; removal causes massive exsanguination.
  • NEVER use unapproved makeshift string or thin wire tourniquets that cut into muscle tissue.
  • NEVER apply turmeric, mud, lime, or coffee powder to bleeding wounds (causes catastrophic septic infection).
When to Contact 999: Blood spurting in pulses, continuous bleeding failing to stop after 10 minutes of direct pressure, or signs of shock (pallor, dizziness).
Professional Care Threshold: Any wound gaping wider than 0.5 cm, wounds caused by rusted metal (tetanus risk), or wounds over joints.
4

Burns and Scalds

Thermal flames, hot liquid scalds, electrical flashes, and corrosive heat.

20-Minute Water Rule
Immediate Safe Actions
  • COOL IMMEDIATELY FOR 20 MINUTES: Place the affected burned area under gentle, cool running tap water for a full 20 minutes continuously. This halts the thermal burn progression into deeper tissue layers.
  • Remove tight rings, wristwatches, bracelets, and belts from the burned area gently before swelling starts.
  • After thorough cooling, cover the burn loosely with clean, non-adherent sterile plastic cling wrap or a clean sterile cloth to prevent airborne bacteria.
  • Keep the patient warm with blankets over non-burned regions to prevent systemic hypothermia.
What NOT to Do (STRICT SAFETY RULES)
  • NEVER APPLY ICE OR ICE-COLD WATER: Extreme cold causes tissue necrosis, frostbite, and severe vasoconstriction that deepens tissue death.
  • NEVER APPLY TOOTHPASTE, OIL, BUTTER, GHEE, RAW EGGS, OR POWDER: Domestic kitchen pastes trap heat inside the flesh and cause severe bacterial contamination.
  • NEVER BURST OR PUNCTURE BLISTERS: Intact blister skin is the body's natural sterile biological barrier against infection.
  • NEVER pull off clothing that is stuck to charred burned flesh. Cut loosely around adhering fabric.
When to Contact 999: Any burn larger than the patient's palm, all burns to the face, hands, feet, joints, or groin, electrical burns, or burns causing white charred skin.
Professional Care Threshold: All second-degree blistering burns in infants or seniors require direct burn unit admission.
5

Electric Shock

High and low voltage electrical contacts, severed lines, and appliance shorts.

Isolate Power First
Immediate Safe Actions
  • DO NOT TOUCH THE VICTIM: Touching a casualty still in contact with an electric current will electrocute the rescuer instantly.
  • Immediately switch off the main circuit breaker, pull the wall plug, or shut off the main fuse board.
  • If power cannot be cut, use a dry, non-conductive object (dry wooden broom handle, dry PVC pipe, rolled cardboard) to push the wire away while standing on dry insulated material.
  • Once separated, check breathing immediately. If not breathing, begin CPR without delay and dial 999.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER touch the person with bare hands or metallic items while current is live.
  • NEVER use damp wood or wet objects to push electrical lines away.
  • NEVER approach high-voltage downed utility cables (stay at least 10 metres away until utility workers declare line dead).
When to Contact 999: Immediately in all electric shock incidents. Internal cardiac arrhythmias can develop hours after the shock.
Professional Care Threshold: Every victim of electric shock requires an emergency 12-lead hospital ECG examination.
6

Choking and Foreign Body Airway Obstruction

Food bolus or foreign object trapped in trachea causing asphyxiation.

Airway Clearance
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Encourage Coughing: If the person can speak, cough forcefully, or breathe, encourage them to cough. Do not interfere with coughing.
  • Severe Choking (Cannot speak or breathe, grasping throat):
    • Deliver 5 sharp back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand, leaning the person forward.
    • If back blows fail, deliver 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver): Stand behind, place a fist just above the navel, grasp with other hand, pull inward and upward sharply.
    • Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until object is expelled or patient loses consciousness.
  • Infant Choking: Place infant face down along your forearm supporting head, deliver 5 gentle back blows, then turn face up and deliver 5 two-finger chest thrusts.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER perform blind finger sweeps in the mouth (you risk pushing the foreign object deeper into the vocal cords).
  • NEVER perform abdominal thrusts on infants under 1 year old (risk of liver laceration; use chest thrusts instead).
  • NEVER give a choking person water to drink.
When to Contact 999: If the obstruction is not expelled after two cycles of 5 back blows and 5 thrusts, or if victim loses consciousness.
Professional Care Threshold: Anyone who receives abdominal thrusts must be checked at hospital for internal rib or organ trauma.
7

Fainting and Unconsciousness

Syncope, diabetic coma, traumatic brain injury, and neurological collapse.

Airway Preservation
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Simple Faint (Vasovagal): Lay the person flat on their back and elevate legs 30 cm above heart level. Loosen tight collars. Ensure fresh air circulation. Recovery usually occurs in under 60 seconds.
  • Unresponsive but Breathing: Place the person into the Recovery Position: roll them onto their side, tilt head gently back to keep airway open, bend upper leg at right angle to prevent rolling forward.
  • Monitor breathing continuously. If breathing stops, roll flat onto back and start CPR immediately (30 chest compressions to 2 rescue breaths).
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER pour water over an unconscious person's face or force water/liquids into their mouth (leads to fatal pulmonary aspiration).
  • NEVER slap, shake, or shout at an unresponsive casualty.
  • NEVER place pillows under the head of an unconscious person lying on their back (forces chin toward chest, obstructing airway).
When to Contact 999: If the person remains unconscious for longer than 60 seconds, fell and struck their head, or has irregular noisy breathing.
Professional Care Threshold: Every episode of unexplained collapse requires diagnostic evaluation for seizures, stroke, or heart rhythm disorder.
8

Poisoning and Chemical Exposure

Ingestion of pesticides, caustic household cleaners, drug overdoses, or toxic gas inhalation.

Toxicological Emergency
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Call Poison Control / 999 Immediately: Identify the chemical container, brand label, chemical ingredients, and estimated quantity ingested.
  • Inhaled Poison (Gas/Fumes): Evacuate casualty to fresh open air immediately, protecting yourself with a respirator.
  • Skin Chemical Splash: Douse skin under copious running water for at least 15 minutes, removing contaminated clothes.
  • Eye Chemical Exposure: Flush open eye with gentle clean water for 20 minutes from the inner bridge of nose outward.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER INDUCE VOMITING unless instructed by a qualified doctor or poison center. If a caustic acid, alkali, or petroleum product was swallowed, vomiting burns the esophagus a second time.
  • NEVER give raw milk, raw eggs, or mustard water as home remedies.
  • NEVER perform mouth-to-mouth resuscitation without a protective pocket barrier if toxic chemical residue is around victim's lips.
When to Contact 999: Immediately upon discovery of any toxic ingestion, overdose, or chemical contact.
Professional Care Threshold: Bring the original chemical container or medicine bottle to the emergency room for targeted antidote selection.
9

Drowning and Water Emergencies

Submersion in rivers, ponds, flood currents, and drainage ditches.

Rescue From Land First
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Follow the Rule: REACH, THROW, ROW, GO:
    • 1. Reach: Extend a bamboo pole, branch, or clothing from safe dry land.
    • 2. Throw: Cast a buoyant lifebuoy, rope, or empty plastic canister.
    • 3. Row: Use a boat or canoe if available.
    • 4. Go: Only trained certified lifeguards with buoyant gear should ever enter water.
  • Once rescued to land, check breathing immediately. If not breathing, deliver 5 rescue breaths first, followed by 30 chest compressions, repeating CPR cycles.
  • Wrap casualty in warm dry blankets to counter rapid hypothermia.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER jump into water to rescue a struggling drowning person if you are untrained (a panicked drowning victim will pull the rescuer under).
  • NEVER waste time trying to "drain water from the lungs" by holding the person upside down or pushing on stomach (delays oxygen delivery and causes stomach contents to choke lungs).
When to Contact 999: Immediately when a person goes missing or is submerged in water.
Professional Care Threshold: Anyone rescued from near-drowning must be admitted to hospital due to the risk of "secondary drowning" (delayed pulmonary edema) up to 48 hours later.
10

Fire and Smoke Inhalation

Carbon monoxide, hydrogen cyanide poisoning, and thermal airway burns.

Toxic Smoke Escape
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Evacuate immediately into open fresh air. Crawl on hands and knees beneath smoke layers.
  • Cover mouth and nose with a damp cloth if available to filter coarse particulates.
  • Provide high-flow oxygen at the earliest moment through paramedics.
  • Rest the patient in a sitting position to ease labored breathing.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER re-enter a smoke-filled room or building for any reason.
  • NEVER underestimate smoke exposure: airway swelling can close the windpipe hours after leaving the scene.
  • NEVER ignore coughing, black soot around nostrils, or singed nasal hairs.
When to Contact 999: Dial 999 immediately for Fire Rescue and Ambulance dispatch.
Professional Care Threshold: Anyone trapped in an enclosed fire requires mandatory hospital observation and blood carboxyhemoglobin screening.
11

Snakebites and Animal Bites

Venomous elapid/viper bites and mammalian rabies exposure risks.

Immobilize Limb
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Snakebite: Keep the patient calm and completely still. Any muscular movement speeds venom circulation through lymph channels.
  • Immobilize the bitten limb using a rigid wooden splint and loose bandage, keeping the limb at or slightly below heart level.
  • Transport immediately to the nearest Upazila Health Complex or District Hospital with Anti-Venom Serum (AVS).
  • Dog / Animal Bite: Wash wound immediately with running water and soap for a full 15 minutes to wash out rabies virus. Proceed to hospital for Anti-Rabies Vaccine (ARV) and Immunoglobulin.
What NOT to Do (FATAL SNAKEBITE MYTHS)
  • NEVER CUT THE BITE WOUND WITH RAZOR BLADES (causes massive uncontrolled bleeding and tissue necrosis).
  • NEVER ATTEMPT TO SUCK VENOM WITH YOUR MOUTH (ineffective and poisons rescuer via oral micro-cuts).
  • NEVER APPLY TIGHT TOURNIQUETS OR ROPE LIGATURES (causes gangrene and limb amputation).
  • NEVER visit traditional faith healers (Ozha/Kobiraj); delay in anti-venom administration is the leading cause of death.
When to Contact 999 / Hospital: Immediately. Note the physical description of the snake if safe to observe (do not attempt to capture or kill it).
Professional Care Threshold: Every venomous snakebite and mammalian animal bite requires certified hospital care.
12

Mental Health Crises and Suicide Emergencies

Acute psychological distress, severe panic attacks, and imminent self-harm risk.

Compassionate Crisis Care
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Stay Present: Never leave a person in acute suicidal crisis alone. Stay with them in a calm, quiet environment.
  • Remove Lethal Means: Discreetly remove access to toxic medications, pesticides, sharp knives, ropes, and firearms.
  • Listen Without Judgment: Speak in a calm, grounded voice. Acknowledge their emotional pain: "I am here with you, and you are safe right now."
  • Connect immediately with verified crisis hotlines: Kaan Pete Roi (+880-1779554391) or National Emergency 999.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER dismiss or debate their feelings ("You are overreacting" or "Others have it worse").
  • NEVER promise absolute secrecy if the person is threatening immediate self-harm.
  • NEVER leave the person unattended to go search for help alone.
When to Contact 999: If the individual has self-injured, ingested harmful substances, or is on an elevated ledge.
Professional Care Threshold: Connect with psychiatric and psychosocial emergency units immediately following initial stabilization.
13

Missing Children and Vulnerable People

Lost children in crowds, disoriented elderly persons with Alzheimer's/dementia, and disaster separation.

Golden Hour Response
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Search Water Hazards First: Check nearby ponds, open wells, ditches, rivers, swimming pools, and construction pits immediately.
  • Alert Hub Management: If in a shopping mall, transit station, or ferry terminal, demand an immediate security lockdown of exits and public address broadcast.
  • Dial 999 & Child Helpline 1098: Report the disappearance immediately. There is NO 24-hour waiting requirement for children or vulnerable persons!
  • Provide police with recent clear color photos, exact clothing descriptions, and identifying birthmarks.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER wait hours hoping the missing child will return on their own before calling authorities.
  • NEVER assume someone else is looking after the vulnerable individual.
  • NEVER post personal home phone numbers publicly without official police liaison guidance (prevents extortion scams).
When to Contact 999: Within the first 15 minutes of an unsuccessful local search.
Professional Care Threshold: File an immediate General Diary (GD) and national law enforcement alert dispatch.
14

Other Urgent Crises: Anaphylaxis & Fractures

Acute systemic allergic shock and traumatic closed/open bone fractures.

Urgent Intervention
Immediate Safe Actions
  • Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis): Look for throat swelling, hives, lip puffiness, and wheezing. Help victim administer their autoinjector (EpiPen) into the outer mid-thigh if available. Call 999 instantly.
  • Suspected Fractures: Support the injured limb in the position found. Splint the joint above and below the fracture using rolled newspapers or cardboard and loose ties.
  • Cover open fracture wounds with sterile dry dressings without pushing protruding bones back inside.
What NOT to Do
  • NEVER attempt to straighten, align, or "set" a fractured bone yourself.
  • NEVER massage or violently rotate a joint that could be dislocated.
  • NEVER delay emergency transport for an anaphylactic patient even if an autoinjector was given.
When to Contact 999: Breathing difficulty during allergy, loss of consciousness, open protruding bone fractures, or absent pulse in limb.
Professional Care Threshold: Orthopedic surgical evaluation and radiological imaging required for all suspected fractures.
